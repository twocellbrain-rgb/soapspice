I checked their current calculator and documentation. Their calculator takes oil weight + oil composition + superfat, then calculates the required NaOH and liquid. They also account for 97% NaOH purity for solid soap.

What Bramble Berry appears to be doing

Their calculator is essentially working from a standard liquid amount based on the finished recipe, rather than asking you to choose a lye concentration.

Bramble Berry states that a typical recipe uses about 22% liquid, and their water-discounting instructions say to take the calculator's water amount and multiply it by the desired discount.

So, for example, if the calculator gives:

Oils = 1,000 g
NaOH = 135 g
Liquid = 220 g

that's approximately:

$$ 220/1000 = 22\% $$

of the oil weight.

Why this is important for your SoapSpice calculator

If you're trying to reproduce the Bramble Berry-style calculator, you could structure it like this:

Step 1 — Oils → theoretical NaOH

$$ NaOH_{theoretical} = \sum(Oil_i \times SAP_i) $$

Step 2 — Apply superfat

$$ NaOH_{adjusted} = NaOH_{theoretical}\times(1-SF) $$

Step 3 — Account for NaOH purity

If using Bramble Berry's 97% assumption:

$$ NaOH_{actual} = \frac{NaOH_{adjusted}}{0.97} $$

Step 4 — Calculate liquid

A Bramble-Berry-style starting point can be represented approximately as:

$$ Liquid \approx Oils \times 0.22 $$

Then a water discount can be applied:

$$ Liquid_{discounted} = Liquid \times (1-Discount) $$

For example, 1,000 g oils:

22% liquid

$$ 1000\times0.22=220g $$

With a 10% water discount:

$$ 220\times0.90=198g $$

Bramble Berry specifically recommends starting around a 10% discount and says not to exceed 15% for their method.

One important correction to my previous answer

My earlier explanation using lye concentration is the more flexible soap-formulation approach. Bramble Berry's public calculator appears to hide that complexity from the user and gives you the liquid amount automatically.

That's actually a very good approach for SoapSpice.

You could have two modes:

Simple Mode

Enter oils → choose superfat → SoapSpice calculates lye + liquid automatically.

Advanced Mode

Choose lye concentration (e.g. 28–40%) → SoapSpice calculates liquid.

That would make SoapSpice much more powerful than simply copying a fixed water/lye ratio
